
## Types of Creators
Creators usually fall into three main groups based on their follower count. Nano-influencers have small, tight-knit followings (under 10,000) that feel like a group of close friends, making their recommendations super trustworthy. Micro-influencers have mid-sized audiences (10,000 to 100,000) and are great for reaching specific hobbies or niches without spending a ton of money. Macro-influencers have massive followings (over 100,000), which costs more but gets your brand in front of a huge crowd all at once.

## Structuring a Campaign
To keep a creator partnership running smoothly, start with a clear brief—a simple guide that explains your goals, key talking points, and any rules for the post. Next, agree on exact deliverables, such as one Instagram Reel or two TikTok videos. Finally, plug those due dates and go-live times straight into your [[content-calenders]] so your team knows when everything is dropping and can keep your overall posting schedule organized.

## Vetting the Right Partner
Before paying a creator, make sure their followers are real people who actually care about what they post. Look at their [[social-media-metrics]] to check if they get genuine comments and shares, rather than just a high follower count with empty likes. Watching out for fake bot accounts ensures your money goes toward an active audience that will truly pay attention to your brand.
